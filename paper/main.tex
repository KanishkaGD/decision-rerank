\begin{abstract}
System One models, decision models that answer a question about a state with a calibrated probability, have been
proposed as rerankers, and open decision fine-tunes now match the closed original. We ask whether decision
fine-tuning makes a better reranker than reranker fine-tuning of the same backbone, and whether decision models are
at least better calibrated. On six Qwen backbones (0.6B--27B) and ten collections, we compare the zero-shot base
model, its decision fine-tunes (scoring candidates jointly or in isolation) and its reranker fine-tunes, including
one we trained ourselves. Both answers are negative. Reranker fine-tuning wins on every backbone where both exist,
also on collections without training data, while decision fine-tuning stays within a few points of the base model;
joint scoring helps only models trained on long states. Decision models are not consistently better calibrated than
reranker fine-tunes, do not give better sets at a fixed probability cutoff, and our reranker fine-tune, trained with
a pointwise loss on its yes/no logit, is the best calibrated system we tested.
\keywords{Reranking \and Calibration \and System One Models \and Fine-Tuning}
\end{abstract}
