\section{RQ2: Are Decision Models at Least Better Calibrated?}
\label{sec:calibration}

\input{tables/calibration}

Calibration is the main promise of decision models. Table~\ref{tab:calibration} reports it on the judged pairs of TREC
DL 2019--2023, together with the two uses we test.

\paragraph{Asked our question, decision models are well calibrated.}
The untuned base models of 0.6B and 0.8B parameters say ``yes'' to almost everything (ECE 0.750 and 0.617); from 4B
on, they are reasonably calibrated already (0.117 to 0.231). On the small backbones, fine-tuning of either kind
improves on the base model, reranker fine-tuning as much as decision fine-tuning (0.6B: Kev 0.503, Qwen3-Reranker
0.511; 0.8B: Kev 0.336, Prism 0.243). From 4B on, the decision fine-tunes
are better calibrated than the published reranker fine-tunes (ECE: Kev-4B 0.132 vs.\ Prism-4B 0.218, Kev-8B 0.113 vs.\
Qwen3-Reranker-8B 0.257, Clef-Flash 0.112 vs.\ Prism-9B 0.242). The best calibrated systems in the study are \jev{}
(0.075) and Kev-27B (0.076); Kev-27B is also better calibrated than our 27B reranker fine-tune (0.110). Decision
fine-tuning is not the only way there: our 9B reranker fine-tune, which asks no question and was trained with a plain
yes/no loss, is as well calibrated as Clef-Flash (0.111 vs.\ 0.112) and has the lower Brier score (0.157 vs.\ 0.158).

\paragraph{The advantage comes from the question.}
All of this holds for a question that matches the relevance labels. Section~\ref{sec:question} repeats every run with
S1Rank's looser question: the same decision models then lose their advantage over the reranker fine-tunes, while
their ranking hardly moves. The calibration of a decision model is a property of the question it is asked.

\paragraph{Calibration does not buy better passage selection.}
If every passage with $p \ge 0.5$ is kept, a reranker fine-tune keeps the better set on every backbone up to 9B
(e.g.\ $F_1$@.5 of 51.0 for Prism-4B vs.\ 42.6 for Kev-4B, and 50.7 for Prism-9B vs.\ 47.1 for Clef-Flash), although
it is the worse calibrated model on most of them. For the decision models, 0.5 is close to the best threshold of each
collection (regret of 0.9 to 3.1 points), whereas Prism-9B loses 4.0 points and Ours-9B 6.4 against their best
thresholds. A well-placed threshold on a worse ranking still yields the worse set. At 27B, where the two kinds of
fine-tuning rank equally well, the better calibrated Kev-27B keeps the better set (57.2 vs.\ 48.3); our fine-tune is
underconfident and keeps only 7.9 passages per query.

\paragraph{Counting relevant passages.}
The count test asks how many of a query's judged passages are relevant, which a ranking cannot tell. Most decision
models from 4B on beat always guessing the median count (8.6) and the published reranker fine-tunes of their
backbones (Kev-8B 7.6 vs.\ Qwen3-Reranker-8B 12.9, Kev-4B 8.4 vs.\ Prism-4B 11.5). The untuned Qwen3.8-27B counts most
accurately of all (5.8), and Ours-9B counts better than Clef-Flash (7.3 vs.\ 8.1): counting works with a matching
question, with or without decision fine-tuning.

\paragraph{Calibration after the fact.}
Rerankers can also be calibrated after training. Temperature scaling fitted on MS~MARCO dev lowers the ECE of the
worst calibrated rerankers on TREC DL (Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B from 0.511 to 0.326, the Set-Encoder from 0.495 to 0.250)
and changes the others little. Platt scaling makes every reranker worse at the fixed cutoff ($F_1$@.5 of 8.1 or less):
MS~MARCO dev judges about one passage per query, so the fitted probabilities are far too low for the denser judgments
of TREC DL. Calibration is defined only with respect to a notion of relevance and its base rate
(Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion}).
