\section{Ablation: The Wording Decides}
\label{sec:question}

\input{tables/question-ablation}

Decision models are sold as models one can simply ask. So far we asked them one question, whether the passage answers
the query. Table~\ref{tab:question-ablation} repeats every run of a decision or base model with the question of
S1Rank~\cite{S1Rank}. It asks whether the passage is relevant and defines relevance more loosely: the passage
``answers the query or contains information that directly addresses it''. Both wordings are reasonable, and S1Rank's
is the one under which \jev{} was first shown to be a good reranker.

\paragraph{Ranking hardly moves.}
The mean nDCG@10 of the decision fine-tunes changes by $-2.5$ to $+2.6$ points between the two questions (\jev{} joint
60.5 with S1Rank's question vs.\ 59.1 with ours, Kev-27B joint 61.1 vs.\ 60.1). The answer to RQ1 does not depend on
the wording: with S1Rank's question, too, the best decision fine-tune of every backbone up to 9B is 4 points or more
behind the reranker fine-tune.

\paragraph{Calibration moves a lot.}
The ECE drops for almost every model when it is asked our question, often by a factor of two or three: \jev{} joint
from 0.224 to 0.075, Kev-8B joint from 0.243 to 0.113, Kev-27B joint from 0.156 to 0.076. The count error moves with
it (\jev{} joint from 11.3 to 6.3 passages). Asked S1Rank's question, the decision fine-tunes are at best as well
calibrated as the best reranker fine-tune of their backbone (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}): Kev-4B 0.216 vs.\ Prism-4B 0.218, Kev-8B
0.243 vs.\ Qwen3-Reranker-8B 0.257, Clef-Flash 0.140 vs.\ Ours-9B 0.111, Kev-27B 0.156 vs.\ Ours-27B 0.110. No decision
model then beats the median guess in the count test by more than a fraction of a passage (Clef joint 8.4 vs.\ 8.6).
One sentence of the prompt decides the answer to RQ2.

\paragraph{The base models move as much.}
The untuned models gain as much from the wording as the decision fine-tunes (Qwen3-8B from 0.383 to 0.231,
Qwen3.5-9B from 0.283 to 0.165). What makes the probabilities calibrated is a question that matches the definition of
relevance in the labels, not decision fine-tuning. The user of a decision model has to put that definition into words,
and a small change in the words changes the calibration severalfold. A reranker fine-tune has no such knob: its notion
of relevance is fixed by its training labels.
