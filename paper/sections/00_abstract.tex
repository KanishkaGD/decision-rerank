System One models, or decision models, answer a question about an input with a calibrated probability instead of
generated text. A month after the first of them was released, they are already used as rerankers. We test whether
the decision fine-tuning behind them makes a better reranker than plain reranker fine-tuning of the same model. For
six Qwen backbones from 0.6B to 27B parameters and ten TREC DL and BEIR collections, we compare the zero-shot base
model with its decision fine-tunes and its reranker fine-tunes, one of which we train ourselves. On every
backbone where both exist (0.6B to 9B), reranker fine-tuning wins, by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10, also on collections
that no fine-tune was trained on. Up to 9B, decision fine-tuning adds nothing over simply asking the base model; only
the largest decision model improves on its base model, and it is barely ahead of reranker fine-tunes a third of its
size. The calibration that decision models are advertised for
comes from the question, not from the model: asked whether a passage answers the query, they are well calibrated;
asked S1Rank's looser question, the same models lose their advantage over reranker fine-tunes, and the untuned base
models gain as much from the wording. Nor does calibration buy better passage selection: a reranker fine-tune keeps
the better passages at a fixed threshold. If you need a reranker, fine-tune a reranker.
