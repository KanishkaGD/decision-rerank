\section{Decision vs.\ Reranker Fine-Tuning}
\label{sec:reranking}



\paragraph{Reranker fine-tuning wins on every model.}
For all five foundation models where both exist, the reranker fine-tune ranks better than the decision fine-tune in
either mode (Table~\ref{tab:triplets}; e.g.\ 58.7 vs.\ 51.3 for Qwen3.5-4B, and 59.0 for Prism-9B and 59.4 for
Ours-9B vs.\ 54.7 for Clef-Flash). The difference is significant on 46 of the 60 pairs of model and collection, and
the decision fine-tune is never significantly better ($^\ast$). The zero-shot subset shows the same, so the gap does not come from training on
MS~MARCO, and Ours-9B, trained on MS~MARCO alone, matches Prism-9B. Up to 9B, decision fine-tuning hardly changes
the base model (between 3.5 points below and 3.2 points above its average; $^\dagger$). Reranker fine-tuning adds 5 to 32 points. Only
Kev-27B clearly improves on its base model (by 3.7 points, significant on eight collections). It is the best system
in our study (61.1), but only slightly ahead of reranker fine-tunes a third of its size (59.4).

\paragraph{Joint vs.\ isolated.}
Scoring all candidates together helps only models trained on long states, and only a little: Kev-27B gains 1.3
points and \jev{} 1.0 (Table~\ref{tab:context-short}), while Kev-0.6B, Kev-0.8B and Kev-8B lose 10.7 to 13.9 points
($^\ddagger$). Kev-0.8B and Kev-4B were trained on states of at most 7,552 tokens and Kev-27B on up to
32,768~\cite{KevCards} (Kev-8B's card gives no length), while a joint state has about 13k tokens on TREC DL and 40k
or more on BEIR. Beyond that length the model loses track of which passage a question is about (Kev-0.6B joint is at
chance level on DL 2019/2020).
