\section{Are Decision Models Better Calibrated?}
\label{sec:calibration}

\input{tables/calibration}

A model is well calibrated if its probabilities match how often passages are actually relevant; ECE measures the
gap. Calibration is the main selling point of decision models, and the base models are indeed poorly calibrated
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration}; ECE up to 0.750). Decision fine-tuning lowers their ECE on every model, but so does
reranker fine-tuning. Compared with the reranker fine-tune of the same model, the decision fine-tune is worse
calibrated at Qwen3-0.6B (0.579 vs.\ 0.511) and Qwen3.5-0.8B (0.357 vs.\ 0.243), about equal at Qwen3.5-4B (0.216
vs.\ 0.218) and Qwen3-8B (0.243 vs.\ 0.257), and better only at Qwen3.5-9B against Prism (Clef-Flash 0.140 vs.\
0.242). For the same model, Ours-9B is the best calibrated system in our study (0.111), better than every decision
model, including the 27B ones (0.147 and 0.156) and \jev{} (0.224). Good calibration thus comes from a loss that
treats the score as a probability, not from decision fine-tuning. A count test, which compares the sum of a
query's probabilities with its number of relevant passages, agrees: decision and reranker fine-tune tie at 4B and 8B,
and Ours-9B counts best of all systems (7.3; Clef 8.4, Kev-27B 8.5, \jev{} 11.3).

\paragraph{One cutoff for every collection.}
A practical use of calibration is to keep every passage with $p \ge 0.5$, e.g.\ to decide which passages reach a
generator. With this rule, the best reranker fine-tune selects the better set for every model where both exist
($F_1$@.5, e.g.\ Prism-4B 51.0 vs.\ Kev-4B 41.0, Prism-9B 50.7 vs.\ Clef-Flash 46.6). Decision models are also not
the only ones for which 0.5 is close to the best cutoff (low regret): Prism (2.8 to 4.0), bge (3.8) and the base
Qwen3.8-27B (2.4) are as close as Clef-Flash (2.6), Kev-27B (2.8) and Clef (3.1), and closer than \jev{} (6.2). A low ECE alone is not enough, though: Ours-9B is too cautious on TREC DL, keeps only 8.7 passages per query and has a regret of 6.4.

\paragraph{Calibrated after training.}
Fitted on MS~MARCO dev, temperature scaling lowers every reranker's ECE (e.g.\ Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B from 0.511 to
0.326), but none gets below 0.20. Platt scaling brings all of them to about 0.23 but empties the set at 0.5
($F_1$@.5 of 5.7 to 7.4), because MS~MARCO dev judges only about one passage per query. A well calibrated model is
better, but it need not be a decision model, as Ours-9B shows.
