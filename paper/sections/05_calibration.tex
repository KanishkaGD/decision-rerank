\section{Are Decision Models Better Calibrated?}
\label{sec:calibration}

\input{tables/calibration}

Calibrated probabilities are the selling point of decision models, and the zero-shot base models are indeed poorly
calibrated (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}; ECE up to 0.750). Decision fine-tuning lowers their ECE on every backbone --
but so does reranker fine-tuning, and against the reranker fine-tune of the same backbone the decision fine-tune has
no consistent edge: it is worse at Qwen3-0.6B (0.579 vs.\ 0.511) and Qwen3.5-0.8B (0.357 vs.\ 0.243), on par at
Qwen3.5-4B (0.216 vs.\ 0.218) and Qwen3-8B (0.243 vs.\ 0.257), and better only at Qwen3.5-9B against Prism (Clef-Flash
0.140 vs.\ 0.242). On that backbone, Ours-9B is the best calibrated system of the study (0.111), below every
decision model including the 27B ones (0.147--0.156) and \jev{} (0.224): calibration follows from a pointwise
probabilistic loss, not from decision fine-tuning.

\paragraph{One cutoff for every collection.}
Calibration should allow one threshold for all collections, e.g.\ to decide which passages reach a generator.
Keeping every candidate with $p \ge 0.5$, the best reranker fine-tune yields the better set on every backbone where
both exist ($F_1$@.5, e.g.\ Prism-4B 51.0 vs.\ Kev-4B 41.0, Prism-9B 50.7 vs.\ Clef-Flash 46.6). A low regret, i.e.\
0.5 being close to each collection's best cutoff, is no privilege of decision models: Prism (2.8--4.0), bge (3.8)
and the untuned Qwen3.8-27B (2.4) match Clef-Flash (2.6), Kev-27B (2.8) and Clef (3.1), and beat \jev{} (6.2). High
regrets come from rerankers whose scores were never meant as probabilities (Set-Encoder 21.1, monoT5-3B 9.9). Low
ECE alone does not make a good set at 0.5: Ours-9B is underconfident on TREC DL, keeps only 8.7 passages per query
and has a regret of 6.4.

\paragraph{Calibrated after the fact.}
Rescaling a reranker needs labelled data. Fitted on MS~MARCO dev, temperature scaling lowers every reranker's ECE
(e.g.\ Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B 0.511 to 0.326, Set-Encoder 0.495 to 0.250) but none gets below 0.20; Platt scaling brings
all to about 0.23 but collapses the set at 0.5 ($F_1$@.5 5.7--7.4), since MS~MARCO dev judges about one passage per
query and the fitted bias learns that almost nothing is relevant. Post-hoc scaling is no substitute for a calibrated
model -- but that model need not be a decision model, as Ours-9B shows.
