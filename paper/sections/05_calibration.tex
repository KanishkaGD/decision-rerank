\section{Are Decision Models Better Calibrated?}
\label{sec:calibration}

\input{tables/calibration}

Calibrated probabilities are the selling point of decision models, and the zero-shot base models are indeed poorly
calibrated (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}; ECE up to 0.750). Decision fine-tuning lowers their ECE on every backbone --
but so does reranker fine-tuning, and against the reranker fine-tune of the same backbone the decision fine-tune has
no consistent edge: it is worse at Qwen3-0.6B (0.579 vs.\ 0.511) and Qwen3.5-0.8B (0.357 vs.\ 0.243), on par at
Qwen3.5-4B (0.216 vs.\ 0.218) and Qwen3-8B (0.243 vs.\ 0.257), and better only at Qwen3.5-9B against Prism (Clef-Flash
0.140 vs.\ 0.242). On that backbone, Ours-9B is the best calibrated system of the study (0.111), below every
decision model including the 27B ones (0.147--0.156) and \jev{} (0.224): calibration follows from a pointwise
probabilistic loss, not from decision fine-tuning. A count test -- the summed probabilities of a query's judged candidates
as an estimate of how many are relevant -- agrees: decision and reranker fine-tune tie at Qwen3.5-4B (11.5 each) and
Qwen3-8B (12.8 vs.\ 12.9), the reranker fine-tune counts better at the two smallest backbones, and Ours-9B counts
best of all systems (7.3; Clef 8.4, Kev-27B 8.5, \jev{} 11.3).

\paragraph{One cutoff for every collection.}
Keeping every candidate with $p \ge 0.5$ (e.g.\ to decide which passages reach a generator), the best reranker
fine-tune yields the better set on every backbone where both exist ($F_1$@.5, e.g.\ Prism-4B 51.0 vs.\ Kev-4B 41.0,
Prism-9B 50.7 vs.\ Clef-Flash 46.6). A low regret (0.5 close to each collection's best cutoff) is no privilege of
decision models: Prism (2.8--4.0), bge (3.8) and the untuned Qwen3.8-27B (2.4) match Clef-Flash (2.6), Kev-27B (2.8)
and Clef (3.1), and beat \jev{} (6.2); only rerankers whose scores were never meant as probabilities fall far behind
(Set-Encoder 21.1). Low ECE alone is not enough: Ours-9B is underconfident on TREC DL, keeps only 8.7 passages per
query and has a regret of 6.4.

\paragraph{Calibrated after the fact.}
Fitted on MS~MARCO dev, temperature scaling lowers every reranker's ECE (e.g.\ Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B 0.511 to 0.326)
but none gets below 0.20; Platt scaling brings all to about 0.23 but collapses the set at 0.5 ($F_1$@.5 5.7--7.4),
since MS~MARCO dev judges about one passage per query. Post-hoc scaling is no substitute for a calibrated model --
but that model need not be a decision model, as Ours-9B shows.
