\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

System One models, or decision models, answer a yes/no question about a structured state with a calibrated
probability, named after the fast, intuitive mode of thinking~\cite{Kahneman2011}. They are marketed
widely, and public experiments show that the closed \jev{} reranks BM25 candidates as well as strong cross-encoders
when asked whether each candidate is relevant~\cite{S1Rank,JevRerank,LanceDB2026,Elastic2026}. Open decision
fine-tunes of Qwen models (Clef~\cite{Clef}, Kev~\cite{Kev}) followed within weeks
\FLAG{release dates of \jev{}, Clef and Kev}, and in our experiments Kev-27B matches \jev{} as a reranker
(Sect.~\ref{sec:reranking}). That points not to a breakthrough behind a closed model, but to a training paradigm --
decision fine-tuning with RLCD \FLAG{cite and expand RLCD} -- that anyone can apply to an open backbone, and whose
merits for retrieval are untested.

The fair question is therefore not closed vs.\ open, but decision fine-tuning vs.\ the established alternative,
reranker fine-tuning, on the same backbone. We hold the backbone fixed and compare \emph{triplets}: the base model
asked zero-shot, its decision fine-tune (scored jointly or in isolation) and its reranker fine-tune, on six Qwen
backbones from 0.6B to 27B parameters and ten collections. Since calibrated probabilities are the decision models'
distinctive feature, we also ask whether they at least deliver that:

Both answers are negative. (1) On every backbone where both exist (0.6B--9B), reranker fine-tuning beats decision
fine-tuning, also on the collections without training data; decision fine-tuning stays within a few points of the
untuned base model, and scoring all candidates jointly only helps models trained on long states. (2) Decision
fine-tunes are not systematically better calibrated than reranker fine-tunes of the same backbone, do not give a
better set at a fixed cutoff, and a reranker fine-tune with a pointwise loss on the yes/no logit -- our own Qwen3.5-9B
fine-tune -- is the best calibrated system we tested. Our contributions are the controlled triplet comparison with
both decision modes, the calibration analysis with a fixed cutoff and post-hoc scaling, and a reranker fine-tune of
Qwen3.5-9B that ranks like the best published one and is calibrated better than any decision model. Code and raw
