\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Cross-encoders are a well-established way to rerank the candidates of a first-stage
retriever~\cite{Nogueira2019,Nogueira2020monoT5}, and open large language models fine-tuned for ranking are the
current state of the art~\cite{Ma2023RankLLaMA,Zhang2025Qwen3Embedding,Zhang2026Prism}. A recent alternative are
\emph{System One models}, also called decision models. They answer a question about an input with a probability for
each possible answer instead of generating text, in the spirit of fast, intuitive
thinking~\cite{Kahneman2011,TypeSafe2026Jev}. TypeSafe AI released the first of them, \jev{}, on 15 September 2026,
trained with a method it calls RLCD (reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions) and has not
published~\cite{TypeSafe2026Jev}. Within weeks, \jev{} was reported to be a competitive
reranker~\cite{S1Rank,JevRerank} and was evaluated or integrated in retrieval
systems~\cite{Elastic2026,LanceDB2026,Hindsight2026}. Open decision models followed: Kev by the end of
September~\cite{Kev} and Cloudflare's Clef on 1 October~\cite{Clef}. They are fine-tuned from Qwen backbones, the
same backbones that dedicated rerankers such as Qwen3-Reranker~\cite{Zhang2025Qwen3Embedding} and
Prism~\cite{Zhang2026Prism} are built on.

The main selling point of decision models is that their probabilities are calibrated. Reranking, however, depends
on the order of the scores, not on their values: any monotone transformation of the probabilities leaves the ranking
unchanged, so calibration alone does not make a better reranker. The reports so far compare \jev{} with rerankers
built on other, mostly smaller models. They cannot tell how much of its effectiveness is due to decision
fine-tuning and how much to the capability of the underlying language model. We argue that a fair evaluation of
decision models as rerankers has to control for the backbone and compare against a reranker fine-tune of the same
backbone. It should also test whether the advertised calibration holds for relevance, and whether it is of any use
once the ranking is worse. We ask:

Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} shows the design. For six Qwen backbones from 0.6B to 27B parameters, we compare three
variants of the same model: the base model asked zero-shot, its decision fine-tunes (Kev and Clef, in two modes) and
its reranker fine-tunes. No reranker fine-tune of the 27B backbone has been released, so we train one ourselves, and
a second one at 9B as a control, both on MS~MARCO alone with standard losses. All variants rerank the same BM25
candidates of TREC DL 2019--2023 and five BEIR collections, and all return a probability of relevance per passage.
Every decision and base model is asked the same, plain question: does the passage answer the query? An ablation
replaces it with the longer question of S1Rank~\cite{S1Rank}, the first public evaluation of \jev{} as a reranker.

Both answers are negative.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Reranker fine-tuning wins.} Up to 9B, the reranker fine-tune ranks better than the decision fine-tune
  on every backbone, by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10 on average, significantly on 48 of 60 pairs of backbone and collection
  and never significantly worse. This holds on collections that no fine-tune was trained on.
  \item \emph{Decision fine-tuning adds nothing up to 9B.} It stays within $-2.5$ and $+0.7$ points of simply asking
  the base model, while reranker fine-tuning adds 6 to 30 points. Only Kev-27B clearly improves on its base model,
  and our own 27B reranker fine-tune, trained with a public recipe, ranks as well as it does.
  \item \emph{Calibration does not rescue decision models.} With our question they are better calibrated than the
  published reranker fine-tunes from 4B on; with S1Rank's question they are not. Our reranker fine-tune of
  Qwen3.5-9B, trained with a plain yes/no loss, is as well calibrated as the best decision model of its size. And up
  to 9B, a reranker fine-tune keeps the better set of passages at a fixed threshold. Only at 27B is the decision
  fine-tune better calibrated than our reranker fine-tune.
\end{itemize}
In short: if you need a reranker, fine-tune a reranker. Decision models may have their uses, but as released so far,
reranking is not one where they beat a model trained for it. Code, raw model outputs (a probability per query and
