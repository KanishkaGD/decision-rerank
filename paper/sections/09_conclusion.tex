\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Do System One models make better rerankers? No. On the same backbone, plain reranker fine-tuning beats decision
fine-tuning up to 9B parameters, by 5 to 29 points nDCG@10, and decision fine-tuning buys nothing over simply asking
the base model. At 27B, where Kev is the best system in our study, a reranker fine-tune trained with a public recipe
ranks as well, although Kev-27B is better calibrated. Calibration, the main promise of decision models, is largely a property of the question rather
than of decision fine-tuning: when the question matches the relevance labels, even the base models become much better
calibrated, and when it does not, the decision models lose their edge over reranker fine-tunes. Our reranker
fine-tune, which asks no question, is as well calibrated as the best decision model of its size, and up to 9B no
decision model kept better passages at a fixed threshold than the reranker fine-tune of its backbone. If you need a reranker, fine-tune a
reranker.
