\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Backbones and variants.}
We study six Qwen backbones: Qwen3-0.6B and 8B, Qwen3.5-0.8B, 4B and 9B, and Qwen3.8-27B. Each is evaluated in up to
three variants (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). The \emph{base model} is the released model, asked the question in its chat
format with thinking off and scored by the probability of ``yes'' as the first answer token. The \emph{decision
fine-tunes} are Kev~\cite{Kev,KevCards} (LoRA on the pretrained checkpoints up to 9B, a full fine-tune of the
post-trained 27B model) and Clef-Flash (9B) and Clef (27B)~\cite{Clef}, each run in isolated and joint mode
(Sect.~\ref{sec:decision-models}). We call them decision fine-tunes because they fine-tune an open model to give
\jev{}'s kind of output; they are not reproductions of RLCD, which is unpublished~\cite{KevCards}. The \emph{reranker
fine-tunes} are Qwen3-Reranker~\cite{Zhang2025Qwen3Embedding}, built on the same pretrained checkpoints as Kev, and
Prism~\cite{Zhang2026Prism}, both scored by the probability of ``yes'' against ``no''.

\paragraph{Our reranker fine-tunes.}
No reranker fine-tune of Qwen3.8-27B has been released, so we train one ourselves (\emph{Ours-27B}), from exactly the
checkpoint that Kev-27B and Clef start from. As a control, we train a second one with the same recipe on Qwen3.5-9B
(\emph{Ours-9B}), where it can be compared with Prism-9B. The recipe is public and deliberately plain: LoRA ($r=32$)
in Qwen3-Reranker's prompt format, one epoch over 100k MS~MARCO training queries~\cite{Nguyen2016MSMARCO}, each with
one relevant passage and 15 hard negatives (eight from a dense retriever, seven from BM25), and a ranking loss
(InfoNCE over the group) plus a yes/no classification loss (binary cross-entropy of every passage's score). The second
term gives the model a meaningful $P(\textit{yes})$. We removed training queries that match a test or development
topic and chose the checkpoint on 200 MS~MARCO dev queries by a rule fixed in advance. No decision data and no
hyperparameter search are involved. Qwen3-Reranker, Prism and our fine-tunes saw MS~MARCO in training; Kev's training
data contain neither MS~MARCO nor BEIR~\cite{KevCards}.

\paragraph{The question.}
Every decision and base model is asked one question per passage: ``Does document \texttt{\{doc\}} answer the
\texttt{query}?'' It matches the relevance labels we evaluate against (on TREC DL, a passage counts as relevant if it
answers the query, from grade~2). We fixed this question before running the experiments, but did not choose it on
development data, and we report every result with it. The only other wording we ran is the question of
S1Rank~\cite{S1Rank}: ``Is document \texttt{\{doc\}} relevant to the \texttt{query}? Relevant means it answers the
query or contains information that directly addresses it.'' It is the subject of the ablation in
Sect.~\ref{sec:question}. Reranker fine-tunes ask no question of this kind; they keep their own prompt.

\paragraph{Collections and candidates.}
We use TREC DL
2019--2023~\cite{Craswell2020DL19,Craswell2021DL20,Craswell2021DL21,Craswell2022DL22,Craswell2023DL23} on the
MS~MARCO v1 and v2 passages~\cite{Nguyen2016MSMARCO} and five BEIR collections~\cite{Thakur2021BEIR}: TREC-COVID,
NFCorpus, SciFact, Touch\'e and DBPedia. None of the fine-tunes was trained on TREC-COVID, Touch\'e or DBPedia, which
form the \emph{zero-shot subset} (ZS). Every system reranks the BM25 top-100 of Pyserini~\cite{Lin2021Pyserini}; these
are the candidates of S1Rank~\cite{S1Rank} and of earlier
comparisons~\cite{Sun2023RankGPT,Pradeep2023RankZephyr,Abdallah2025HowGood}, whose published numbers we use where we
did not rerun a system.

\paragraph{Other systems.}
For context, we report BM25, \jev{} 1.13.0 (closed, in both modes), \laya{}~\cite{Laya} (isolated only, as its
context is too short for a joint state), monoT5-3B~\cite{Nogueira2020monoT5}, bge-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{Chen2024BGEM3},
the Set-Encoder~\cite{Schlatt2025SetEncoder}, RankZephyr~\cite{Pradeep2023RankZephyr} (run by us on TREC DL) and
RankGPT-4~\cite{Sun2023RankGPT} (published numbers only).

\paragraph{Ranking.}
We measure nDCG@10 and test differences with paired $t$-tests over queries, Holm-corrected across the ten
collections ($p < 0.05$). Per backbone, we test the reranker fine-tune against the decision fine-tune with the best
average (in its better mode), that decision fine-tune against the base model, and joint against isolated mode.

\paragraph{Calibration and its uses.}
We compute the expected calibration error (ECE, 15 equal-width bins)~\cite{Guo2017Calibration} and the Brier score
(the mean squared error of the probability against the binary label, which also rewards separating relevant from
non-relevant passages) on the judged query--passage pairs of TREC DL 2019--2023, the only collections that also judge
many non-relevant passages; a passage counts as relevant from grade~2. We test two uses of the probabilities. A
\emph{fixed cutoff} keeps every candidate with $p \ge 0.5$; we report the $F_1$ of this set (unjudged passages count
as non-relevant), its size, and its \emph{regret}: the $F_1$ at the best threshold of each collection, chosen
afterwards, minus the $F_1$ at 0.5. The \emph{count} test sums a query's probabilities over its judged passages, which
estimates how many of them are relevant, and reports the mean absolute error of this estimate in passages; always
guessing the median count is the trivial baseline. Reranker fine-tunes are also calibrated after the fact, with
temperature and Platt scaling of their logit fitted on 1,000 MS~MARCO dev queries, never on a test collection.

\paragraph{Sanity checks.}
Our BM25, monoT5-3B and RankZephyr runs reproduce published numbers~\cite{Pyserini2CR,Abdallah2025HowGood} within 0.2
points nDCG@10. \jev{} in joint mode, asked S1Rank's question, reproduces S1Rank's numbers~\cite{S1Rank} within 0.5
points on four of five collections (1.5 below on TREC-COVID).
