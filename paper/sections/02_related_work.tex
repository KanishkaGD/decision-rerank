\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{LLM-based rerankers.}
Neural rerankers are trained to score query--passage pairs from relevance labels, first with encoder or
sequence-to-sequence models~\cite{Nogueira2019,Nogueira2020monoT5} and more recently with open large language
models~\cite{Ma2023RankLLaMA,Zhang2025Qwen3Embedding,Zhang2026Prism}. Qwen3-Reranker and Prism read relevance from the
probability of ``yes'' against ``no'' after a prompt that asks whether the document meets the query, which makes their
output directly comparable to that of a decision model. Other approaches rerank the candidates jointly, either by
generating a ranked list~\cite{Sun2023RankGPT,Pradeep2023RankZephyr} or by letting passages attend to each other, as in
the Set-Encoder~\cite{Schlatt2025SetEncoder}. Large language models can also rank without any reranking-specific
fine-tuning, through relevance prompting~\cite{Qin2024PRP,Zhuang2024BeyondYesNo}, but their effectiveness is sensitive
to how the prompt is worded~\cite{Sun2025PromptVariations}. Abdallah et~al.~\cite{Abdallah2025HowGood} compare many of
these rerankers on the candidates we use.

\paragraph{Decision models in retrieval.}
Besides \jev{}~\cite{TypeSafe2026Jev}, Kev~\cite{Kev,KevCards}, Clef~\cite{Clef} and \laya{}~\cite{Laya} are open
decision models. The evidence on decision models for retrieval is so far limited to reports that are not
peer-reviewed: \jev{} reranking BM25 candidates~\cite{S1Rank,JevRerank,LanceDB2026}, as a reranker in production
search~\cite{Elastic2026,Hindsight2026}, and the open \laya{} as a relevance gate before recall~\cite{Laya,Smriti2026}. These reports
compare \jev{} with existing rerankers, which are built on other models. None compares decision fine-tuning with
reranker fine-tuning of the same backbone, and none asks whether the calibration of the probabilities pays off.

\paragraph{Calibration of relevance scores.}
Neural networks tend to be overconfident, and temperature scaling after training is a simple
remedy~\cite{Guo2017Calibration}. Neural rankers are no exception~\cite{Penha2021Calibration}. Common ranking losses
are invariant to shifting the scores of a query, so rankers trained with them can order passages well without being
calibrated~\cite{Penha2021Calibration,Yan2022ScaleCalibration}; scores can be calibrated during or after
training~\cite{Guo2017Calibration,Yu2025ExplainThenRank}. Calibrated probabilities matter wherever a score is read on
an absolute scale, for example to cut off a ranked list~\cite{Bahri2020Choppy}. This is the use we test in RQ2.
