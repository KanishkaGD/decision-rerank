\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Triplets.}
For each of six foundation models (Qwen3-0.6B and 8B, Qwen3.5-0.8B, 4B and 9B, and Qwen3.8-27B) we compare three
variants.
\emph{Base}: the released model, asked the decision models' question in its chat format (thinking off) and scored by
the probability of ``yes'' as the first answer token. \emph{Decision fine-tune}: Kev~\cite{Kev,KevCards} (LoRA on the
pretrained checkpoints up to 9B, a full fine-tune at 27B), Clef-Flash (9B) and Clef (27B)~\cite{Clef}.
\emph{Reranker fine-tune}: Qwen3-Reranker~\cite{Zhang2025Qwen3Embedding} (on the same pretrained checkpoints as Kev)
and Prism~\cite{Zhang2026Prism}, scored by the probability of ``yes'' against ``no''; none is public for 27B. For
Qwen3.5-9B we add our own (\emph{Ours-9B}), trained without any decision data: LoRA ($r=32$), one epoch over 100k
MS~MARCO training queries with one relevant and 15 hard negative passages each, a ranking loss (InfoNCE) plus a
yes/no classification loss (binary cross-entropy). We removed training queries that match a test or development
topic and chose the checkpoint on 200 MS~MARCO dev queries. Qwen3-Reranker, Prism and Ours-9B saw MS~MARCO; Kev's
training data contain neither MS~MARCO nor BEIR~\cite{KevCards}.

\paragraph{Collections and candidates.}
TREC DL 2019--2023~\cite{Craswell2020DL19,Craswell2021DL20,Craswell2021DL21,Craswell2022DL22,Craswell2023DL23}
(MS~MARCO v1/v2 passages~\cite{Nguyen2016MSMARCO}) and TREC-COVID, NFCorpus, SciFact, Touch\'e and DBPedia from
BEIR~\cite{Thakur2021BEIR}. TREC-COVID, Touch\'e and DBPedia have no training split in any fine-tune's data and form
the \emph{zero-shot subset} (ZS). Every system reranks the BM25 top-100 of Pyserini~\cite{Lin2021Pyserini}, the
candidates of S1Rank~\cite{S1Rank}, RankGPT~\cite{Sun2023RankGPT}, RankZephyr~\cite{Pradeep2023RankZephyr} and
Abdallah et al.~\cite{Abdallah2025HowGood}, whose published numbers we use where we did not rerun a system.

\paragraph{Decision models.}
All decision models get S1Rank's question~\cite{S1Rank}, fixed in advance (``Is document \texttt{\{doc\}} relevant
to the \texttt{query}? Relevant means it answers the query or contains information that directly addresses it.''),
with a state that holds all 100 candidates (\emph{joint}) or one (\emph{isolated}). Besides the triplets we run
\jev{} 1.13.0 (closed), \laya{}~\cite{Laya} (isolated only, as its context is too short),
monoT5-3B~\cite{Nogueira2020monoT5}, bge-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{Chen2024BGEM3}, the
Set-Encoder~\cite{Schlatt2025SetEncoder} and RankZephyr (DL only). For RQ2 we also ask a stricter question on
DL 2019--2023, ``Does document \texttt{\{doc\}} answer the \texttt{query}?'', which matches the relevance grade we
score; it was fixed before our runs but not chosen on development data.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
nDCG@10, with paired $t$-tests over queries, Holm-corrected across the ten collections ($p < 0.05$). Expected
calibration error (ECE, 15 bins)~\cite{Guo2017Calibration} on the judged pairs of DL 2019--2023 (relevant from grade
2), the only collections that also judge non-relevant passages. \emph{One cutoff}: we keep every candidate with
$p \ge 0.5$ and report the $F_1$ of this set (unjudged passages count as non-relevant). \emph{Sanity checks}: our BM25 matches
Pyserini's reproductions~\cite{Pyserini2CR}, monoT5-3B and RankZephyr are within 0.2 points of their published DL
2019/2020 numbers~\cite{Abdallah2025HowGood}, and \jev{} joint is within 0.5 of S1Rank's~\cite{S1Rank} on four of
five shared collections (1.5 below on TREC-COVID).
